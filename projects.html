2025

  • •
    Climate Change and Sanitation: Assessing Resilience and Emissions (Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, 2025). Conducted quantitative analysis of household surveys to develop two statistical models and contributed to two journal articles. For the first paper, explanatory and confirmatory factor analysis were applied to enhance the robustness of the analysis. For the second paper, a logistic model was implemented to identify the main factors that increase or reduce the odds that a household is negatively affected by flooding.
  • •
    Costing and financial analysis of rural water schemes in Laos (UNICEF EAPRO, 2025). Estimated the cost of delivering basic water services in rural provinces and assessed the financial sustainability of the schemes. Results suggest that OPEX recovery can be achieved and that there is still fiscal space to improve water quality under the current community tariff. However, full cost recovery cannot be achieved, and the rehabilitation of water scheme infrastructure depends on external funding.
  • •
    Estimating and monitoring Official Development Assistance expenditure in the WASH Sector in Southeast Asia (UNICEF EAPRO, 2025). Processed and analysed the Credit Report System database (6 million observations) to assess ODA investments — including loans, grants, and other financial flows — from development agencies and multilateral organisations to the WASH sector across Southeast Asia from 2013 to 2023. Based on this analysis, a dashboard was developed in Power BI to enhance decision-making processes and highlight external financial gaps for the sector, providing policymakers and stakeholders with data-driven insights to support investment strategies.

2024

  • •
    Emission Measurements for Improving Sanitation in Indonesia (KIAT, 2024–2026).

    Developed a complex model in Python to estimate Indonesia's sanitation emissions. As COD (Chemical Oxygen Demand) and N-influent (Nitrogen influent) are key drivers of methane and nitrous oxide production, the pathways of these elements were modelled across sanitation systems to estimate GHG emissions produced at each stage of the sanitation chain.

    This quantitative model provided the first comprehensive estimate of GHG emissions from Indonesia's sanitation sector, highlighting the significant contribution of sanitation systems to national emissions and the need to strengthen mitigation efforts to support the transition to a low-carbon economy. It also offers recommendations to reduce emissions, such as integrating solar energy into treatment plants.

  • •
    Real Water Indonesia (2024). Developed two logistic models to identify the main predictors for functionality and water quality in Indonesian villages that were part of the PAMSIMAS programme. A Lasso regression was included to improve prediction accuracy.
  • •
    Estimating greenhouse gas (GHG) emissions from the sanitation chain in Khulna, Bangladesh (SNV Bangladesh, 2024). Analysed the sanitation system in Khulna and adapted Johnson's (2022) methodology to estimate GHG emissions for each stage of the sanitation chain. Results indicate that Khulna emits 57 million CO₂e annually, with the containment and treatment stages being the primary sources of these emissions.

    WASH Contributions to Community Resilience (UNICEF EAPRO, 2023–2024). Supported the development of monitoring guidance for gathering evidence on the linkages between WASH and broader community resilience to climate change.

2019–2021

  • •
    Fostering data interoperability in the Peruvian government to improve education, health and sanitation services for children (Ministry of Social Inclusion, Peru).

    Collaborated with a multidisciplinary team to address the lack of access to public services for children in rural areas. The primary objective was to identify children who had not received essential services, including education, healthcare, and clean water — crucial information for local authorities responsible for delivering these services.

    An innovative technological platform was developed to facilitate data interoperability across ministries. Implementation required extensive coordination across offices and ministries, defining governance structures, and systematising databases to optimise processing efficiency. The successful pilot focused on five rural districts, identifying all children under five who had not yet received essential services, and sharing this information with social workers for follow-up visits to each family.

  • •
    Improving public spending in the health sector (Ministry of Economy, Peru).

    Led a project examining healthcare spending within the Peruvian health system using administrative data. After processing more than one million health service records, consistent patterns were uncovered indicating a considerable percentage of medical staff failing to complete their designated working hours despite receiving full monthly salaries.

    This absenteeism resulted in an annual cost of approximately PEN 90 million (USD 23 million) to the Peruvian government. In response, the Ministry of Economy and the Ministry of Health (MINSA) agreed to implement a computer-based system for monitoring and scheduling medical staff shifts. Additionally, the project successfully advocated for resources to establish a dedicated data centre equipped with high-performance computers, to serve as a hub for processing and analysing administrative data and enabling evidence-based decision-making.
